# What Makes Employees Leave? A Plain-Language Summary

**Company:** Salt Corporation
**Question:** Which things about an employee are linked to them leaving the company?
**Employees looked at:** 1,000, of whom 392 left (about 39 out of every 100)

---

## The Short Version

- **Job satisfaction is the one clear warning sign.** Unhappy employees left far more often than happy ones.
- **The year someone joined also shows a link,** but it needs a closer look before we draw conclusions.
- **Everything else showed no real link to leaving.** This includes department, job position, pay level, age, gender, employment type and benefits.

---

## How the Scoreboard Works

For each factor (like department or age group), we split employees into groups. We then worked out what share of each group left.

We then compared the group with the most leavers against the group with the fewest. The difference between the two is called the **gap**.

| Column                            | What it means                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Factor**                        | The thing we looked at (for example, department)                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Highest Group / Highest Value** | The group where the largest share of people left, and that share (as a percentage)                                                                                                                           |
| **Lowest Group / Lowest Value**   | The group where the smallest share of people left, and that share                                                                                                                                            |
| **Gap**                           | The highest share minus the lowest share, in percentage points. A big gap means the factor makes a real difference to who leaves. A small gap means it makes little difference.                              |
| **Smallest Group Size**           | The number of people in the smallest group. Small groups give shaky percentages, because just a few people can move the result a lot.                                                                        |
| **Luck Check**                    | How likely it is that the gap happened by pure chance. A **low** number means the gap is probably real. A **high** number means the gap could easily be luck. We treat anything below 0.05 as probably real. |

**Reading a "Luck Check" example:** a value of 0.6 means there's a good chance the gap is just luck. A value of 0.01 means it's unlikely to be luck.

## What Each Result Means

### Job Satisfaction: the clearest link

- More than half of the employees who rated their satisfaction 1 or 2 left (59% and 53%).
- About a quarter of those who rated it 3, 4 or 5 left (26% to 27%).
- The big drop happens between a score of 2 and a score of 3. Beyond 3, higher satisfaction made little extra difference.
- The chance that this is luck is close to zero.
- **In plain terms:** an employee who is unhappy is much more likely to leave. Keeping people at a satisfaction level of 3 or above is where most of the benefit is.

### Joining Date: worth a closer look

- Employees who joined in 2018 left more often (48%) than those who joined in 2020 (36%).
- The gap is probably real, but there are two reasons to be careful:
  - People who joined in 2018 have simply had more years in which they could leave.
  - Because we checked ten factors, one of them can look real by luck alone.
- **In plain terms:** this is a lead worth following up, not a firm conclusion.

---

## Things to Keep in Mind

- **This shows links, not causes.** Unhappy employees leave more often, but this data can't prove that unhappiness is the reason.
- **The Benefits Enrolled result is not final.** It still counts the 210 employees with no recorded answer as their own group. The company's rules count them as "No", so this row needs to be recalculated.
- **Small groups give shaky numbers.** The smallest group in any comparison was 161 people or more, which is large enough to trust the main results.
- **We are comparing the highest and lowest groups only.** The gap ignores the groups in between, so it is a quick guide, not the whole picture.

---

## What We Recommend Next

1. Check whether the 2018 joiners are less satisfied than the others. If so, joining year may just be job satisfaction in disguise.
2. Check whether any other factor matters once job satisfaction is held steady (compare unhappy and happy employees separately).
3. Look directly at whether the company is losing its top performers.
4. Focus any retention effort on employees who report low satisfaction.
